1. What Information Do We Collect?

In Short: We collect the display name you enter, an anonymous app identifier, and information about the parties you create or join and the songs played in them.

Information you provide to us

  • Display name. When you join a party, you enter a name that is shown to the other members of that party. It does not need to be your real name.
  • Party settings. When you host a party, we store the settings you choose (such as maximum song length, skip-vote threshold, turn order, and whether submissions are anonymous), the music service you use, and the party's join code.
  • Party activity. While you are in a party, the songs you pick, the search terms you enter, your skip votes, and playback status are sent between the devices in that party.

Information created when you use the app

  • Anonymous app identifier. When you first open the app, it creates a random, anonymous user ID for your installation. This ID is not tied to your name, email, or phone number, and it is used to recognize your device across parties.
  • Song-play history. Each time a song you picked is played, we record which song was played, which music service it came from, and the party session it belongs to.
  • Network information. Like any internet service, our hosting provider and the music services receive your device's Internet Protocol (IP) address when the app connects to them.

Music service authorization (hosts only)

If you host a party, you may connect Spotify or Apple Music so the app can search for songs and control playback.

  • Spotify. The app asks for permission to read and control your playback state and currently playing track, and to read basic account details (such as your country and subscription level) so searches return songs available to you. The access tokens Spotify issues are stored only on your device, in its secure storage (the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore). They are never sent to us. You can remove them by long-pressing the music provider when setting up a party, by revoking access in your Spotify account settings, or by uninstalling the app.
  • Apple Music. The app asks for your permission, through your device, to search the Apple Music catalog and play songs. We do not receive your Apple ID credentials.

We do not access your music library, playlists, or listening history on either service.

Information we do not collect

We do not collect:

  • your email address, phone number, or password
  • your location
  • your contacts, photos, camera, or microphone
  • advertising identifiers
  • payment information

The app contains no advertising, no third-party analytics, and no cross-app tracking.

Sensitive information

We do not process sensitive information.

2. How Do We Process Your Information?

In Short: We process your information to run your parties, keep the Services secure, and comply with the law.

We process your personal information only to:

  • Run parties. This covers creating and finding parties by join code, keeping the queue, turn order, skip votes, and playback state in sync across everyone's devices, and tracking which songs each member has played.
  • Enable interaction between party members. We show your display name and song picks to the other members of your party.
  • Control music playback. The host's device sends search and playback requests, including search terms entered by guests, to the host's music service.
  • Keep the Services secure. We use your information to prevent abuse and fix problems.
  • Comply with legal obligations. We use your information to respond to lawful requests.

3. When and With Whom Do We Share Your Personal Information?

In Short: We share information only with the service providers that make the app work and with the other members of your party. We do not sell your information.

  • Supabase (hosting provider). Party data, display names, song-play history, and live party activity are stored on or transmitted through servers operated by Supabase, which processes this data on our behalf.
  • Spotify and Apple Music. If the host connects a music service, the host's device sends search terms (including those entered by guests) and playback commands to that service. Each service handles this data under its own privacy policy: Spotify Privacy Policy, Apple Privacy Policy.
  • Other party members. Everyone in a party can see each member's display name, who the host is, whose turn it is, and the song that is playing. Unless the host turns on anonymous submissions, members can also see who picked each song.
  • Business transfers. We may share or transfer your information in connection with a merger, sale of assets, financing, or acquisition of all or part of our business.
  • Legal requirements. We may disclose information if required to do so by law or in response to valid legal process.

4. How Long Do We Keep Your Information?

In Short: We keep party information until you ask us to delete it.

Display names, party records, and song-play history are stored until you ask us to delete them. Live party activity, such as search terms and skip votes, is relayed between devices and is not stored by us. Spotify access tokens remain on your device until you remove them or uninstall the app.

To request deletion, see "How can you review, update, or delete the data we collect from you?" We may keep information longer if the law requires it.

5. How Do We Keep Your Information Safe?

In Short: We aim to protect your personal information through organizational and technical security measures.

Data sent between the app, our hosting provider, and the music services is encrypted in transit. However, no electronic transmission over the Internet or information storage technology can be guaranteed to be 100% secure, so we cannot promise or guarantee that hackers, cybercriminals, or other unauthorized third parties will not be able to defeat our security and improperly collect, access, steal, or modify your information. Although we will do our best to protect your personal information, transmission of personal information to and from our Services is at your own risk.

8. Controls for Do-Not-Track Features

Most web browsers and some mobile operating systems and mobile applications include a Do-Not-Track ("DNT") feature or setting you can activate to signal your privacy preference not to have data about your online browsing activities monitored and collected. At this stage, no uniform technology standard for recognizing and implementing DNT signals has been finalized. As such, we do not currently respond to DNT browser signals or any other mechanism that automatically communicates your choice not to be tracked online. If a standard for online tracking is adopted that we must follow in the future, we will inform you about that practice in a revised version of this Privacy Notice.

California law requires us to let you know how we respond to web browser DNT signals. Because there currently is not an industry or legal standard for recognizing or honoring DNT signals, we do not respond to them at this time.
